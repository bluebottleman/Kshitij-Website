Products I worked on through the years

  1. Untangling 20 years of sales data nobody wanted to touch 2025–2026 ▾

    Vanguard ID Systems

    Marketing Associate, Sept 2025 – Feb 2026

    The aim:

    Vanguard ID Systems was running sales on an aging, offline system with no real CRM — and previous attempts to fix it, even with outside vendors involved, hadn't worked. The goal was to move to an AI-assisted, cloud-based CRM that could actually handle sales, marketing, and reporting in one place.

    My part:

    I started by studying the existing data structure — customers, parent companies and their subsidiaries, product quantities, margins — and worked with leadership to decide what mattered enough to carry forward. I rebuilt an equivalent structure in HubSpot, then cleaned the underlying data by hand using custom Excel macros before importing it, so the new system launched on data worth trusting instead of the same mess moved somewhere new.

    Why I loved it:

    I'm a data enthusiast at heart, and this was a genuinely hard puzzle — twenty years of tangled records nobody had wanted to touch. Untangling it into something a whole team could rely on every week was exactly the kind of problem I go looking for.

    What it took:

    The data went back to 2006 — over two decades of weekly sales records, customer IDs that had drifted or duplicated over time, and errors baked in from an earlier cleanup attempt that had made things worse, not better. AI tools couldn't do the core of this work; too much depended on judgment only a person could make — is this contact still with the company five years later, who owns this account now. In the end, I cleaned and migrated roughly 20,000 records — about five months of accurate, usable history with responsible contacts and companies attached.

    Tools I used:

    HubSpot, Excel (custom macros), ChatGPT, draw.io

  2. Cutting a month-long approval cycle down to two weeks 2023–2024 ▾

    Tata NeuCard

    Senior Design Consultant (Contract), 2023–2024

    The aim:

    NeuCard needed to move faster — campaigns and acquisition efforts that used to take months of budget cycles and approvals were bottlenecking growth, and the in-app experience needed real usability work, not just visual polish.

    My part:

    I worked directly with the product and growth teams to identify where the in-app experience was creating friction, then improved those flows. I also introduced AI-assisted asset generation into the workflow, which cut both time and budget on production without sacrificing quality.

    Why I loved it:

    Getting to sit at the intersection of product and growth — not just designing what users saw, but influencing how fast the whole team could move — was exactly the kind of cross-functional work I look for.

    What it took:

    Projects that historically needed months of budget approvals were restructured into two-week iterative cycles. Over 2 million cards were sold during this period.

    Tools I used:

    Figma, Midjourney, ChatGPT, Adobe Creative Suite, Microsoft 365

  3. Designing for first-time borrowers in India's tier 3 and 4 cities 2020–2021 ▾

    Billbolo

    Product & UX Design Consultant, 2020–2021

    The aim:

    Billbolo was a Buy Now Pay Later app built specifically for tier 3 and 4 Indian cities — markets with far less exposure to formal credit and BNPL products than metro users, so the product needed to earn trust and clarity from the very first screen, not just look modern.

    My part:

    I ran market and design research into this user base — how tier 3/4 audiences related to credit, what confused or worried them about BNPL, and where existing apps assumed too much familiarity — then designed the first draft of the onboarding flow and homepage screens around those findings.

    Why I loved it:

    Designing for a genuinely underserved, under-researched audience during Covid, when most fintech design was still copy-pasting metro-city patterns, forced me to actually question defaults instead of inheriting them.

    What it took:

    The research reshaped some fairly basic assumptions about the onboarding flow — where a metro-first BNPL app might lean on speed, this one needed to lean on clarity and reassurance first. The first-draft onboarding and homepage screens came directly out of that shift.

    Tools I used:

    Figma, Adobe Creative Suite

  4. Building an AR teleportation sphere before AR was a thing 2018–2019 ▾

    FlippAR

    Design Consultant, 2018–2019

    The aim:

    FlippAR was a Bangalore travel-tech startup building augmented reality experiences for tourists — the app needed to turn static monuments and travel moments into something genuinely interactive.

    My part:

    I redesigned the mobile app experience and built several of its signature AR features — a "teleportation sphere" letting users step into a different location entirely, and 3D digital replicas of Indian monuments (some modeled inside and out) that users could place and walk into in AR space. I also built the app's scanner feature, which recognized a monument from a photo and geotag, then surfaced contextual information — including details as specific as a worn inscription or a recently broken feature.

    Why I loved it:

    Travel and new tech are two of my favorite things, and this let me work on both at once, with a team that was genuinely excited to push what AR could do before most people had a clear idea what it was for.

    What it took:

    These were ambitious, technically involved builds for a startup — running design sprints, testing prototypes, and iterating quickly. The app performed well enough to earn partnerships with major names in Bangalore's travel industry, including the state tourism department and the international airport.

    Tools I used:

    Unity, ARCore, Blender, Figma, Adobe Creative Suite, Microsoft 365, Invision

  5. My first job: a bank's UX and a family's privacy, at once 2017 ▾

    Sankey Business Solutions

    Associate Consultant, 2017

    The aim:

    My first job — hired for design sensibility and English fluency, handling two international clients simultaneously: a UX/UI overhaul for a large bank's website, and a home-safety app built around a GPS/camera/sensor ecosystem.

    My part:

    For the banking project, I researched competitors and design approaches to shape a UX that would carry across a large number of pages and flows. For the home-safety app, I tried out comparable products myself to understand what worked, find the gaps competitors were missing, and design around real privacy considerations for a whole family's experience — not just one user.

    Why I loved it:

    Two genuinely different research problems, on my very first job, was a real trial by fire — and a home-safety product built to protect an entire family was a meaningful thing to be trusted with early on.

    What it took:

    Both clients approved the initial proposals, but development constraints stalled things before they shipped. I moved on to a new opportunity shortly after — a decision that opened the door to the FlippAR chapter that followed.

    Tools I used:

    Adobe Creative Suite, Microsoft 365, Invision

  6. Building this very site by pairing Figma with Claude 2026 ▾

    kshitijpatil.com

    Designer & Creative Director, 2026

    The aim:

    I wanted this portfolio to feel like something I'd actually built by hand — not a template, not a page builder — while being realistic about my own front-end coding limits.

    My part:

    I designed every page frame-by-frame in Figma, then worked with Claude in an ongoing back-and-forth to turn that design into real, working code — describing what I wanted, reacting to what it built, and iterating live until it matched what was in my head. This section included.

    Why I loved it:

    It's a genuinely different way of building — creative direction stays fully mine, but the implementation becomes a real-time conversation instead of a queue of dev tickets. Being able to redirect something mid-build, instantly, changed how I thought about what "designing" even means.

    What it took:

    Dozens of rounds of feedback — layout tweaks, real copy and images replacing placeholders, custom interactions like the tic-tac-toe game and the sound effects, and a fair amount of "no, more like this" until it felt right.

    Tools I used:

    Figma, Claude
