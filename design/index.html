  1. 🪤 Trapped ▾

    Trapped — a personal art series

    Illustrated in Procreate

    The concept:

    Trapped is a series about a feeling that has no walls you can point to — the sense of being confined not by a room or a cage, but by expectations, fears, and rules that only hold power because we've agreed they do. Each piece gives that invisible confinement a physical shape.

    My approach:

    Every piece started with a single figure and one question: what would it look like if the thing trapping them was as literal as the feeling itself? From there, each illustration found its own visual metaphor for a body caught between what's expected of it and what it actually feels.

    Why I made it:

    It's the closest I've gotten to using art as a diary instead of a deliverable — no client brief, no brand guidelines, just a feeling I kept circling back to until it had a shape.

    Tools I used:

    Procreate

  2. 👀 Human Interactions ▾

    Human Interactions — a personal art series

    Illustrated in Procreate

    The concept:

    Three scenes about the strange, small connections that happen without a word — a boy coming home to a laptop that seems to wait for him the way a dog waits by the door, a present-day update on an old kind of loyalty; a taxi ride where the driver is somewhere else entirely, and the only thing you actually share is the meter ticking upward between you; a rickshaw ride where driver and passenger catch each other mid-glance, both quietly suspicious of the other for no real reason at all.

    My approach:

    Each piece starts from a moment most people forget the second it's over, and asks what it would look like if it actually mattered — connection hiding in the parts of the day nobody thinks to look at twice.

    Why I made it:

    I wanted to prove to myself that a moment doesn't need to be dramatic to be worth painting — sometimes the quietest exchanges are the most honest ones.

    Tools I used:

    Procreate

  3. 🐞 Love Bugs ▾

    Love Bugs — a personal art series

    Illustrated in Procreate

    The concept:

    A small, ongoing set of ladybugs going through whatever the day throws at them — heat, cold, rain — and staying exactly as close through all of it. Love less as a grand gesture, more as just showing up, in every condition.

    My approach:

    Same two characters, different weather each time — a simple visual formula that lets the "withstanding it together" idea do all the talking without needing to explain itself.

    Why I made it:

    It's a small, low-stakes way of saying something I actually believe — the strongest kind of love isn't the loudest, it's the one that's just consistently there.

    Tools I used:

    Procreate

  4. 🦎 Chameleon ▾

    Chameleon — a personal art series

    Illustrated in Procreate

    The concept:

    A set of pieces about the strange habits two people pick up from loving each other long enough — inside jokes, shared mannerisms, finishing each other's sentences — reimagined as literal camouflage, two colors blending until you can't tell where one starts and the other ends.

    My approach:

    I took a chameleon's actual survival trait — blending in to disappear — and flipped it into something romantic: disappearing into someone else not out of fear, but because you've grown that close.

    Why I made it:

    It's about the specific, unglamorous kind of intimacy that only shows up after a long time with someone — less "love at first sight," more "we've somehow become the same person."

    Tools I used:

    Procreate

  5. 💭 Concept Art ▾

    Concept Art — a personal series

    Illustrated in Procreate

    The concept:

    A loose collection of scenes where the rules are just slightly off — a giant, gentle sea creature sharing a quiet moonlit moment with a kid on a rooftop, a world where the ordinary logic of things doesn't quite apply. The "wrongness" is the whole point.

    My approach:

    Each piece starts with one impossible idea and gets built out just far enough to feel like it could be real, without ever fully explaining itself.

    Why I made it:

    Concept art is the closest thing I have to daydreaming on purpose — no client, no brief, just chasing whatever image showed up first.

    Tools I used:

    Procreate
