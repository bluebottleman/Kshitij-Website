  3. Turning a budget tier headed for discontinuation into a hit 2022–2023 ▾

    Treebo Hotels (now Treebo Hospitality Ventures)

    Senior Design Consultant (Brand & Performance Marketing), 2022–2023

    The aim:

    Scale brand awareness and revenue through performance marketing, while also solving a real reputation problem in Treebo's budget hotel segment, which was collecting poor reviews tied to unavoidable budget-property constraints.

    My part:

    Reporting to the Director and VP of Growth & Marketing, I led a small team of designers and copywriters creating performance assets, using Meta and Google Ads analytics to learn what offers and visuals actually converted. Treebo's YouTube had stalled — a legacy batch of music-session videos drove views but no bookings, and skippable agency ads weren't landing with an audience actively tuning ads out. I rebuilt the channel around a video-first, micro-influencer strategy and custom travel-trail content, and improved WhatsApp-based communication with shareable PDF guides that hotel owners and travelers could pass along within their own community.

    Why I loved it:

    Getting to combine hard performance-marketing numbers with a brand that was actively expanding was a rare mix of analytical rigor and room to try new formats.

    What it took:

    The brand grew from roughly 10K to 27K followers during this period, expanded its hotel portfolio, and partnered with major hospitality brands. The YouTube pivot alone drove a 28% year-over-year lift, a 9% rise in repeat bookings, 3.6 million impressions, and 210,000+ direct engagements. (See the separate Itsy by Treebo entry for the budget-tier rebrand from this same period.)

    Tools I used:

    Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads / Analytics, Adobe Creative Suite

  6. Making a loud, physical festival feel loud through a phone screen 2020–2021 ▾

    OML

    Senior Designer (Brand & Digital Marketing), BVS, 2020–2021

    The aim:

    Launch a new, fully virtual season of BVS — a nationwide hip-hop dance and rap battle competition — reimagined for a pandemic-era, digital-only audience.

    My part:

    As senior designer, I managed a team of junior designers plus an external agency, shifting the brand almost entirely into digital assets: loud fonts, loud music, motion and haptics designed to make static content feel alive on social platforms. My team also edited and processed contestant video/audio submissions for social-ready posting, and handled the extra layer of approvals required for celebrity and brand-ambassador content.

    Why I loved it:

    Solving "how do you make a loud, physical, in-person festival feel loud through a phone screen" was a genuinely hard creative problem, and seeing it click for the youth audience it was built for was the best part.

    What it took:

    The season scaled from a previous footprint of 5–6 major cities to a fully nationwide, boundary-less open call — thousands of entries narrowed to 96 finalist artists across four specialist crews (rap, hip-hop, breaking, popping, graffiti), fronted by 2 film-star brand ambassadors and 4 celebrity team captains, over roughly 5 months of digital engagement. Running creative tests with the marketing team taught us two concrete things: visuals that gave brand ambassadors the most space outperformed ones balanced toward the team-captain celebrities, and our Instagram AR filters saw more use when kept minimal — heavier filters lagged and hurt the experience. Heavy back-and-forth with PR teams on celebrity approvals and a steep motion-design learning curve, compressed into a short timeline, came with the territory.

    Tools I used:

    Adobe Creative Suite, Microsoft 365

  7. Building a 50-comedian festival's identity, city by city 2019–2020 ▾

    OML

    Senior Designer (Festival Marketing), The Circuit Comedy Festival, 2019–2020

    The aim:

    Launch and market a nationwide comedy festival featuring over 50 comedians across major Indian cities — Delhi, Bangalore, Pune, Mumbai.

    My part:

    Over roughly three months, I built the full design library needed for the campaign — export-ready for both digital (social) and print (banners, flexes) — largely solo for the first month before a junior team joined to help execute against the guidelines I'd set.

    Why I loved it:

    A genuinely nationwide creative rollout, with real design-system discipline required to keep everything consistent across a huge number of formats and cities.

    What it took:

    Testing communications across audience segments taught a real lesson in restraint: banner copy capped at 7–8 words, leading with the comedian's face, outperformed heavier-copy versions for established names — recognition earned the click, copy came second. Budding comedians without that built-in recognition needed the opposite, leaning on copy to carry context a face alone couldn't yet. Momentum was building well behind that insight — strong audience response across social channels — before COVID hit and the event had to be cancelled outright. The work itself held up; the timing didn't.

    Tools I used:

    Adobe Creative Suite, Microsoft 365
